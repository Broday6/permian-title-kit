The operating model

  • In-house core: a land manager who owns the title product menu and the tracker; one or two senior title landmen who review and QC rather than abstract; a GIS/data analyst; and, if you build, one engineer who owns the pipeline and the fraction engine.
  • Contract brokers for courthouse-heavy counties (late-index counties, plant visits, heirship legwork). Give each one a seat in your system and your runsheet template so the work comes back in your format.
  • Title attorneys for drilling, division order and acquisition opinions, and for every legal-effect call the software flags.
  • One rule: before anyone opens an index, check the title register for existing work on that tract. Duplicate runs are the most common waste in land departments.

RolloutThe first 90 days

Days 1–30: Standardize
  • Adopt the five products, the runsheet schema, the file-naming key and the QC gate on this page.
  • Inventory every opinion, runsheet and abstract you own. Register each by tract with its certified-through date.
  • Open TexasFile and CourthouseDirect TVP Plus accounts. Get BLM Login.gov access for MLRS reports.
  • Load the GIS base: RRC surveys and wells, GLO and University Lands leases, BLM CadNSDI, NMSLO status, OCD wells.
  • Build and test the fraction engine. Pick 20 already-opined tracts as the gold set.
Days 31–60: Pilot
  • Run the vendor bake-off on the gold set: Enverus Courthouse + Tracts, Energy Domain TitleLab, and one young AI title tool.
  • Stand up the in-house extraction path (OCR + vision model + schema + review screen) on the same 20 tracts.
  • Score instrument recall, field accuracy, decimal match and review minutes per instrument.
  • Start the name-variant library and corporate-succession table.
Days 61–90: Production
  • Put live deals through the winning path with Tier A / Tier B review.
  • Turn on the KPI dashboard: cycle time, cost per tract, QC defects, auto-accept rate.
  • Start monthly pay-deck reconciliation against your computed decimals.
  • Launch the curative tracker with owners and due dates.
  • Make the build-vs-buy call based on the bake-off numbers, not on the demos.
