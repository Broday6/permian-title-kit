New MexicoThree sovereigns, one spacing unit

A Delaware Basin horizontal in Eddy or Lea often crosses federal, state and fee tracts. Each one is examined from its own source, then reconciled into one unit. New Mexico has no Marketable Record Title Act and no dormant mineral act, so you examine from sovereignty, and old fractional heirs never drop out of the chain.

Federal tracts (BLM): run them in this order

  1. Normalize to PLSS

    For example, "Sec 15, T23S, R31E, NMPM, NE/4". Join it to BLM CadNSDI polygons. Watch for the irregular lots along north and west township lines and the Texas line.

  2. Confirm mineral status

    Check the Master Title Plat and Historical Index, the MLRS land-status layers and the BLM GLO patent. Stock-Raising Homestead (1916) and 1914 Act patents put federal minerals under private surface all over Eddy, Lea and Chaves. Rule out NMSLO state minerals as well.

  3. Pull every case on the lands from MLRS

    Run the geographic case-recordation report by township, range and section (reports.blm.gov/reports/MLRS). A Login.gov account unlocks unredacted reports. This lists leases, communitization agreements (CAs), units and bonds.

  4. Read each lease's serial register page

    Note dates, disposition, record-title and operating-rights percentages, and the action history: approved assignments, segregations, CA or unit commitment, production determinations, suspensions, termination and reinstatement.

  5. Order the case file

    Request it from the BLM New Mexico State Office public room in Santa Fe, or by FOIA. MLRS doesn't reliably show depth limits on operating rights, ORRI owners or the royalty rate. Those are only in the lease and assignment documents.

  6. Pull each CA and unit

    Exhibit B of a CA lists record title, operating rights and ORRI owners by tract, which makes it one of the best title sources there is. Until BLM approves the CA, federal royalty is paid lease by lease and the division of interest is wrong.

  7. Search the county records

    ORRIs, term assignments, farmouts, JOA memoranda, mortgages, probates and divorces are often recorded in the county but never filed with BLM. Corporate mergers and name changes often appear only at BLM. A complete federal chain needs both sources reconciled.

  8. Check OCD, set the royalty, confirm the bond

    Pull C-102 dedication plats, pooling orders and production from OCD. Set the federal royalty by the lease's issue date (table below). Confirm the operator's bond coverage.

Federal assignment rules that break chains
  • Record-title and operating-rights assignments both need BLM approval. File within 90 days of execution. The assignment takes effect the first of the month after BLM has the complete filing.
  • ORRIs are filed with BLM but not approved.
  • A partial assignment of record title can split the lease into a new serial number.
  • BLM approval doesn't give constructive notice under New Mexico's recording act, so record the assignment in the county too.
  • The 2024 leasing rule raised minimum bonds and renumbered parts of 43 CFR 3100. A June 2026 proposed rule would roll those bonds back, but it isn't final. Check every regulatory cite against the current eCFR.

State tracts (NMSLO)

  • Search SLOConnect by lease, township/range or account. Note the lessee of record, status, lease form, royalty and assignment history. Order the lease file from Santa Fe or by public records request.
  • Record-title assignments are acknowledged conveyances approved by the Commissioner. The two-person rule means state record title can't sit in more than two persons or entities.
  • Any CA or unit that includes state lands needs Commissioner approval as well as BLM or OCD approval.
  • Leasing on trust lands is governed by 19.2.100 NMAC.

Fee tracts (Eddy, Lea, Chaves, Roosevelt)

  • Start at the patent (BLM GLO Records) or the state patent, and note any mineral reservations.
  • Lea County before 1917: Lea was carved from Chaves and Eddy in 1917. Its earlier chain is in those counties' records.
  • Community property. NMSA 40-3-13 requires both spouses to join any conveyance or mortgage of community real property, and minerals count. A deed signed by one spouse is void unless a power of attorney is recorded. Check marriage indexes and divorce decrees at every link.
  • Heirship. Examiners generally want probate. An affidavit of heirship is supporting evidence, not a cure. The small-estate affidavit (45-3-1201) moves personal property only. For out-of-state estates, file the foreign appointment under 45-4-204 or open an ancillary proceeding.
  • Probate files are in two places. Informal probate is filed with the County Clerk as clerk of the probate court (searchable on Lea's Liveweb). Formal or contested probate and heirship determinations are in District Court: the Fifth Judicial District for Chaves, Eddy and Lea, searchable on NM Case Lookup and re:SearchNM.
  • Acknowledgments. An unacknowledged instrument isn't "of record" (14-8-4), even if the clerk entered it. Defects in acknowledgment and authority are cured 15 years after recording (37-1-28).
  • Adverse possession takes 10 years, color of title and payment of taxes (37-1-22). It is practically unavailable against severed minerals.
  • No county mineral appraisal roll. New Mexico taxes production at the state level, so the Texas shortcut of using the CAD mineral roll to find current owners doesn't exist here.

OCD pooling is title work

Compulsory pooling (NMSA 70-2-17; 19.15.13 NMAC; risk charge up to 200%) runs on your ownership schedule. Under a pooling order, an unleased mineral owner is treated as a 7/8 working interest plus a 1/8 royalty, and is carried subject to the risk charge if they don't elect to participate. Since Dec 1, 2025, every pooling application must include a table of all owners in the proposed unit with notice details for each. An owner your title run missed now shows up in that exhibit, and a notice defect puts the order at risk.

OCD API county codes: Eddy 30-015, Lea 30-025, Chaves 30-005, Roosevelt 30-041. Well files and hearing case files are on OCD Permitting and OCD Imaging.
