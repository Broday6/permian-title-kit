Start hereHow a title shop gets fast without getting sloppy

Title speed depends mostly on the records, then on the people, and only after that on the software. Before you staff a project, know which of your counties have their records imaged online back to the patent and which ones still need a trip to the courthouse or an abstract plant. That one question sets your schedule more than anything else.

Rule 1: Build on prior work

Prior drilling and division order title opinions, base abstracts, operator pay decks and your own old runsheets all carry a "certified through" date. Run a supplemental from that date forward. On a developed Permian section that usually saves most of the courthouse time.

Rule 2: Match the run to the decision

A mineral purchase screen, an acquisition closing and a drilling title opinion need very different amounts of work. Pick the product before anyone opens an index (see the scope tiers below).

Rule 3: One runsheet format, every image linked

Use one runsheet schema, one naming convention and one takeoff format across every landman, contractor and tool. Every row points to the recorded image. If a row has no image behind it, it hasn't been verified.

Rule 4: Machines do the clerical work, people make the calls

Automate index searching, downloads, OCR, first-pass abstracting, fraction math and flagging. Keep people on interpretation, curative and anything an attorney will sign. Landmen run title and prepare ownership reports. Attorneys render opinions.

Where the hours go on a manual run

On a typical full run, the larger share of the hours goes to finding and pulling instruments (index searches, name variants, chasing probates and court records). Abstracting and building the chain take the next share, and ownership math and reports take the rest. Automation pays first in search and pull, then in first-pass abstracting. Of those three, only the math can be fully trusted to a machine, because exact fraction arithmetic is deterministic.

Standard operating procedureThe run, step by step

This is the sequence every tract goes through, whether a contractor in Pecos runs it or a pipeline in the cloud does. The automation section later maps each step to what can be machine-run.

  1. Intake and scoping

    Capture the purpose, product tier, tracts (as written legal descriptions), depths, deadline and budget. Add any base you already have: prior opinions, pay decks, data-room files.

  2. Map the tract before searching

    Plot it on the Texas abstract grid or the New Mexico PLSS. Note every survey or section it touches, overlaps, railroad strips, highways, riverbeds and possible vacancies. Flag state (GLO / Relinquishment Act / University Lands / NMSLO) and federal (BLM) status. Pull existing wells, units and production from the RRC or OCD, since production history tells you which leases are probably held by production.

  3. Find the base

    Look in your files, the operator's, abstract plants and prior opinions. The appraisal district mineral roll shows who is paying tax on a decimal today, which makes a good lead list and cross-check. Record the base's certified-through date. Your search window starts there.

  4. Search every index, both directions

    Search grantor and grantee for every owner and every name variant over their ownership window. Cover deed and Official Public Records, oil and gas records, probate (including other counties and states), district court (divorces, partitions, tax suits, receiverships, quiet title), judgments and abstracts of judgment, federal and state tax liens, lis pendens, plats, and BLM/NMSLO/GLO files where they apply. Where the county indexes by abstract or section, also run a legal-description search to catch misindexed instruments.

  5. Pull and image every instrument

    File each one under a consistent key, such as TX-REEVES-OPR-2019-012345.pdf or TX-MIDLAND-DR-0512-0033.pdf. No image, no row.

  6. Abstract into the runsheet

    One row per instrument: type, execution date, filing date, recording reference, grantor, grantee, legal as written, interest conveyed, reservations and exceptions, lease terms, and notes. AI can draft this. A person verifies it against the image.

  7. Build the chain (takeoff / flowchart)

    Tie every conveyance to a prior vesting. Flag gaps (no probate, name mismatch, lost instrument), over-conveyances (Duhig situations), wild deeds and anything conveying "all my interest in" the county.

  8. Calculate ownership by depth

    Work out mineral, royalty, NPRI (fixed or floating), executive rights, leasehold WI/NRI and ORRIs, tract by tract and depth by depth. Use exact fractions all the way through and round only at the final decimal.

  9. Review lease status

    Check the primary term, extensions, Pugh and retained-acreage clauses, depth limits, shut-in and cessation clauses, and recorded releases. Confirm HBP against RRC or OCD production for the lease or well, not against what the operator says.

  10. Write requirements and curative

    For each defect, give the recommended cure: affidavit of heirship, ratification, correction deed, release, stipulation of interest, probate or muniment, or quiet title. Assign an owner and a due date to each.

  11. QC with a second person

    Check 100% of the math and 100% of the chain links. Re-run a sample of the index searches and check name variants. Reconcile the result to the division order or pay-deck decimal. If it doesn't tie out, it doesn't ship.

  12. Deliver and load

    Deliver the ownership report, runsheet, images, map and requirements. Load owners, tracts, leases and interests into the land system. Update GIS. Log the certified-through date so the next run is a bring-down.

Search the parent county too

Most Permian counties were carved out of Bexar (1876 land-district counties), Tom Green (1887–1903 counties), Presidio (Pecos, and through it Reeves) or El Paso (Culberson). Unorganized counties were attached to a neighbor for recording. Search the parent for anything filed before the organization date. Some counties transcribed the parent's records, which is why an index can start before the county existed.

To confirm a county's current platform, use NETR Online (publicrecords.netronline.com/state/TX/county/<county>) or the clerk's own page. Mineral appraisers (Pritchard & Abbott, Thomas Y. Pickett, Capitol Appraisal and others) are contracted per appraisal district and can change, so check the CAD's mineral roll page.

TexasClassify the section before you search the county

In the Texas Permian, the first title question is how the land left the State. The answer decides whether the State still owns the minerals, holds a free royalty, or holds nothing. Pull the abstract from the GLO Land Grant Database (search by county and abstract number; most files are scanned and free). The class and sale papers tell you which row you're in. The survey name alone won't tell you: alternate railroad sections were often sold later as school land but kept the railroad's block label.

Texas law that changes the ownership math

These are the decisions a runsheet and fraction engine have to respect. The software flags them, and the examining attorney decides them. Check pin cites and any later history before you rely on a case in an opinion.

Heirship and probate in Texas

  • Affidavit of heirship (Est. Code §203.001): after 5 years of record it becomes prima facie evidence. Use disinterested affiants, record it in every county where the minerals are, and calendar the 5-year date. For large interests or disputed families, require a judgment declaring heirship.
  • Muniment of title: a will probated this way makes the order a transfer instrument. Wills generally must be offered within 4 years of death. An unprobated will doesn't pass title.
  • Small estate affidavit: passes only homestead real property. Don't accept it as the source of mineral title.
  • Transfer-on-death deeds (since 2015): search for one before applying intestacy.
  • Intestacy, separate property with spouse and children: the spouse gets a life estate in ⅓ of the real property, including minerals. The children get the remainder plus the other ⅔. Both the life tenant and the remaindermen must sign leases.
  • Community property: it all goes to the surviving spouse only if all the decedent's descendants are also the spouse's. Deaths before Sep 1, 1993 follow the older rule. Always apply the law in force on the date of death.
  • Out-of-state probate: record an authenticated copy in each Texas county (Est. Code ch. 503). Until you do, the chain is broken.
  • Missing owners: a receivership (CPRC §64.091) lets a court-appointed receiver sign the lease. Title needs the whole proceeding file. ClaimItTexas shows which operator holds an owner's proceeds in suspense.

Payment timing under Nat. Res. Code §91.402: first payment within 120 days after the end of the month of first sale, then 60 days (oil) or 90 days (gas). A division order can't amend the lease. Mergers vest property without a deed (Bus. Orgs. Code §10.008), so record the certificate of merger in each county to close the chain.

New MexicoThree sovereigns, one spacing unit

A Delaware Basin horizontal in Eddy or Lea often crosses federal, state and fee tracts. Each one is examined from its own source, then reconciled into one unit. New Mexico has no Marketable Record Title Act and no dormant mineral act, so you examine from sovereignty, and old fractional heirs never drop out of the chain.

Federal tracts (BLM): run them in this order

  1. Normalize to PLSS

    For example, "Sec 15, T23S, R31E, NMPM, NE/4". Join it to BLM CadNSDI polygons. Watch for the irregular lots along north and west township lines and the Texas line.

  2. Confirm mineral status

    Check the Master Title Plat and Historical Index, the MLRS land-status layers and the BLM GLO patent. Stock-Raising Homestead (1916) and 1914 Act patents put federal minerals under private surface all over Eddy, Lea and Chaves. Rule out NMSLO state minerals as well.

  3. Pull every case on the lands from MLRS

    Run the geographic case-recordation report by township, range and section (reports.blm.gov/reports/MLRS). A Login.gov account unlocks unredacted reports. This lists leases, communitization agreements (CAs), units and bonds.

  4. Read each lease's serial register page

    Note dates, disposition, record-title and operating-rights percentages, and the action history: approved assignments, segregations, CA or unit commitment, production determinations, suspensions, termination and reinstatement.

  5. Order the case file

    Request it from the BLM New Mexico State Office public room in Santa Fe, or by FOIA. MLRS doesn't reliably show depth limits on operating rights, ORRI owners or the royalty rate. Those are only in the lease and assignment documents.

  6. Pull each CA and unit

    Exhibit B of a CA lists record title, operating rights and ORRI owners by tract, which makes it one of the best title sources there is. Until BLM approves the CA, federal royalty is paid lease by lease and the division of interest is wrong.

  7. Search the county records

    ORRIs, term assignments, farmouts, JOA memoranda, mortgages, probates and divorces are often recorded in the county but never filed with BLM. Corporate mergers and name changes often appear only at BLM. A complete federal chain needs both sources reconciled.

  8. Check OCD, set the royalty, confirm the bond

    Pull C-102 dedication plats, pooling orders and production from OCD. Set the federal royalty by the lease's issue date (table below). Confirm the operator's bond coverage.

Federal lease issuedRoyalty rateSource

Federal assignment rules that break chains
  • Record-title and operating-rights assignments both need BLM approval. File within 90 days of execution. The assignment takes effect the first of the month after BLM has the complete filing.
  • ORRIs are filed with BLM but not approved.
  • A partial assignment of record title can split the lease into a new serial number.
  • BLM approval doesn't give constructive notice under New Mexico's recording act, so record the assignment in the county too.
  • The 2024 leasing rule raised minimum bonds and renumbered parts of 43 CFR 3100. A June 2026 proposed rule would roll those bonds back, but it isn't final. Check every regulatory cite against the current eCFR.

State tracts (NMSLO)

  • Search SLOConnect by lease, township/range or account. Note the lessee of record, status, lease form, royalty and assignment history. Order the lease file from Santa Fe or by public records request.
  • Record-title assignments are acknowledged conveyances approved by the Commissioner. The two-person rule means state record title can't sit in more than two persons or entities.
  • Any CA or unit that includes state lands needs Commissioner approval as well as BLM or OCD approval.
  • Leasing on trust lands is governed by 19.2.100 NMAC.

Fee tracts (Eddy, Lea, Chaves, Roosevelt)

  • Start at the patent (BLM GLO Records) or the state patent, and note any mineral reservations.
  • Lea County before 1917: Lea was carved from Chaves and Eddy in 1917. Its earlier chain is in those counties' records.
  • Community property. NMSA 40-3-13 requires both spouses to join any conveyance or mortgage of community real property, and minerals count. A deed signed by one spouse is void unless a power of attorney is recorded. Check marriage indexes and divorce decrees at every link.
  • Heirship. Examiners generally want probate. An affidavit of heirship is supporting evidence, not a cure. The small-estate affidavit (45-3-1201) moves personal property only. For out-of-state estates, file the foreign appointment under 45-4-204 or open an ancillary proceeding.
  • Probate files are in two places. Informal probate is filed with the County Clerk as clerk of the probate court (searchable on Lea's Liveweb). Formal or contested probate and heirship determinations are in District Court: the Fifth Judicial District for Chaves, Eddy and Lea, searchable on NM Case Lookup and re:SearchNM.
  • Acknowledgments. An unacknowledged instrument isn't "of record" (14-8-4), even if the clerk entered it. Defects in acknowledgment and authority are cured 15 years after recording (37-1-28).
  • Adverse possession takes 10 years, color of title and payment of taxes (37-1-22). It is practically unavailable against severed minerals.
  • No county mineral appraisal roll. New Mexico taxes production at the state level, so the Texas shortcut of using the CAD mineral roll to find current owners doesn't exist here.

OCD pooling is title work

Compulsory pooling (NMSA 70-2-17; 19.15.13 NMAC; risk charge up to 200%) runs on your ownership schedule. Under a pooling order, an unleased mineral owner is treated as a 7/8 working interest plus a 1/8 royalty, and is carried subject to the risk charge if they don't elect to participate. Since Dec 1, 2025, every pooling application must include a table of all owners in the proposed unit with notice details for each. An owner your title run missed now shows up in that exhibit, and a notice defect puts the order at risk.

OCD API county codes: Eddy 30-015, Lea 30-025, Chaves 30-005, Roosevelt 30-041. Well files and hearing case files are on OCD Permitting and OCD Imaging.

Ownership mathDecimal interest calculator

This calculator works the way your fraction engine should: exact fractions all the way through, rounded to eight places only at the end. Type fractions as written in the instrument (3/16, 1/2 of 1/8, 18.75%). For a horizontal allocation or PSA well, choose how tract participation is set: by acreage, or by completed lateral length in the tract.

Tract participation

NRA is shown on a 1/8 basis (NMA × royalty ÷ 1/8), the convention most mineral buyers price on. Confirm which basis your counterparty uses. An unleased Texas cotenant is paid a share of net proceeds after costs, not a royalty, so price that case separately. A tract fully inside one unit gives the same decimal by acreage as NMA ÷ unit acres × royalty.

AutomationThe title factory: what to automate, in what order

Fully automatic title isn't realistic, and you shouldn't buy anything sold that way. The realistic target is that each landman verifies three to five times more instruments per day. That comes from machines doing the search, the pull, the transcription, the first-pass abstract and all the math, while people accept or correct each field against the image. Reviewing a pre-extracted instrument should take a few minutes, against roughly 15–30 minutes to abstract it by hand. Those are planning estimates; measure your own.

Working starter code

The Permian title kit (private repository Broday6/permian-title-kit) implements this section in tested Python:

  • Ownership: the exact-fraction ledger and unit deck, with NPRIs charged only to the minerals they burden.
  • Records: Tesseract/Textract OCR, Texas and New Mexico legal-description parsers, a map check against RRC and BLM survey layers, index name variants, runsheet red-flag rules and county search plans.
  • Output: live spreadsheets for the runsheet and the deck.
  • AI: a Claude extraction step with quote checking, plus a one-command live smoke test.

Run ptk deck examples/project.json --xlsx deck.xlsx to see a full allocation-well deck built from a fictional takeoff.

Automate in this order (highest return first)

  1. Tract-to-grid mapping and "what's in the box"

    Normalize the legal description, drop it on the RRC survey polygons or BLM CadNSDI, and pull every well, lateral, unit, state/federal lease and production record that touches it. It uses free public data and saves an hour per tract.

  2. Index harvesting and document pull

    Use a licensed aggregator (TexasFile index export at $0.10/row; CourthouseDirect plant search). Run every name variant against both indices, then queue downloads. Stay within the vendor's terms (see guardrails).

  3. The fraction engine

    Use exact rational arithmetic, ownership replayed from the ledger by tract, depth and interest type, tract participation factors and 8-place decimals. It's deterministic, so you can trust it once it's tested. Reconcile its output against pay decks every month.

  4. OCR plus AI first-pass abstracting

    Type-specific extraction into a strict schema, with a verbatim quote and line reference for every field. People verify. This is where most of the speed-up comes from, and where most of the risk is.

  5. Name resolution and the chain-walking loop

    Each new party found becomes a new search, and the loop stops at the patent, the root-of-title date, or a prior opinion's cut-off.

  6. Rule-based red flags and curative drafting

    The rules raise the items in the red-flag table automatically. Draft affidavit of heirship packages, ratifications and stipulations from templates for a person to finish.

How AI title extraction fails

Vision models normalize. They quietly "fix" spellings and fill illegible spots with plausible words and numbers. The damaging cases are swapped fractions (1/2↔1/4, 1/8↔1/3), invented volume and page numbers, and corrected names. The controls:

  • Every field carries a verbatim quote tied to OCR line IDs, checked automatically.
  • A second OCR engine, with any disagreement on digits, fractions, names or dates sent to review.
  • A mismatch between the written fraction and the numeral ("one-half (1/4)") always raises a flag.
  • Ignore the model's self-reported confidence. Measure confidence by agreement between engines.
  • Never use AI super-resolution on a source image. It invents characters.

Review tiers

Tier A: a person always reviews

Anything that changes ownership: conveyed fractions and their basis, reservations and exceptions, parties and capacity on pre-1970 instruments, and every double-fraction, over-conveyance, word/numeral or illegible flag.

Tier B: sample review

Header fields where both engines agree and every validator passed. Spot-check 5–10% and accept the rest. Send every correction into the gold set.

Extraction prompt and schema

Send the page images plus the OCR text with stable line IDs (p3.l17: …), and ask for line IDs and a verbatim quote in every evidence entry. Run a cheap model first to classify and split instruments. Then run a prompt specific to that instrument type, because a lease and a mineral deed need different questions.

You are abstracting a recorded instrument for an oil and gas runsheet in {COUNTY} County, {STATE}.
Inputs: page images, and OCR text where every line has an ID like p2.l14.

Rules:
1. Transcribe and extract only what is on the page. If a value is not on the page, return null.
2. Copy names, fractions, legal descriptions and recording references exactly as written,
   including both the words and the numerals of every fraction. Never correct spelling.
3. If text is illegible, put [illegible] in the quote and add an "illegible" flag. Never guess digits.
4. Every extracted object needs evidence: page, the OCR line_ids, and a verbatim quote.
5. Record each fraction as factors (for "1/2 of 1/8": [1/2, 1/8]) and the basis exactly as stated
   (of the minerals, of grantor's interest, of royalty, of production). Do not decide whether a
   royalty is fixed or floating, whether Duhig applies, or who the heirs are. Only flag it.
6. Flag: written fraction ≠ numeral, double fractions, term language ("for __ years and as long
   thereafter"), "all my interest in __ County" language, a spouse who didn't join, an unclear
   capacity (trustee, executor, attorney-in-fact), interlineations, missing pages, no acknowledgment,
   marginal notations, and conveyances larger than the grantor appears to own.
7. List every reservation, exception, "save and except" and "subject to" clause separately,
   with its full verbatim text.
8. Return JSON that matches the schema exactly. No commentary.

Data model: an ownership ledger, not a spreadsheet

  • Core tables: document (immutable image + hash), instrument, party (as written) linked to a resolved person_entity, and entity_link (aka, fka, merged into, heir of, spouse of, successor trustee) with evidence on every link.
  • Tracts: legal_description → tract (PostGIS geometry) → tract_fragment for save-and-except slices. Plus depth_interval and interest_type (MI, executive, bonus, royalty, NPRI fixed/floating, ORRI, WI, NRI).
  • The ledger, conveyance_event: one row per instrument × grantor → grantee × fragment × depth × interest. It stores fraction factors as NUMERIC numerator/denominator pairs, plus the basis.
  • Ownership on any date is a replay of the events in order. A grantor whose balance goes below zero raises an over-conveyance (Duhig) flag.
  • Leases, units and allocation wells carry their own tract participation factors and the source of each factor: PSA, unit designation or pooling order. RRC acreage forms like the P-16 are regulatory filings, not ownership.

Legal descriptions and GIS

Texas: abstract, survey, block, section

  • Parse with a deterministic grammar first: county, abstract (A-123), survey (T&P, H&TC, GC&SF, PSL, UL…), block (C-21, 33), township (T-2-S), section, aliquots and save-and-excepts. Use the LLM only as a fallback, and check its output against the survey layer.
  • Use the RRC digital map data (free county shapefiles: survey polygons, surface and bottom-hole well locations, lateral arcs). The GLO's statewide survey grid is licensed from a third party and can't be redistributed. Use the RRC version, or license Whitestar.
  • Irregular sections: "N/2 of Section 12" needs a geometric split. Mark it as approximate geometry, and treat the legal text as controlling.
  • The Texas vara is 33⅓ inches. Watch for magnetic bearings and check traverse misclosure on metes-and-bounds tracts.

New Mexico: PLSS

  • Resolve aliquots right to left: "N/2 SE/4" → {NESE, NWSE}. Normalize lots through CadNSDI PLSSIntersected and compare geometry, never strings. "W/2 of irregular Section 6" can equal "Lots 3–7, SE/4NW/4, E/2SW/4."
  • Sources: BLM CadNSDI (NM), NMSLO GIS downloads (UTM 13N, EPSG:26913), OCD GIS hub, BLM MLRS reports.
  • Check each layer's projection file for NAD27 vs NAD83.

The search loop

  1. Seed with the tract and its known owners (the CAD mineral roll in Texas, or the deal file).

  2. Expand every name: initials, nicknames (Jno., Wm., Chas.), married and maiden names, "et ux" forms, trusts and estates, corporate predecessors and successors. Use phonetic keys, because old index books group names by letter codes and misindex often.

  3. Search both directions: the grantor index from acquisition forward, including after death; the grantee index back toward the root. Limit each search to the window when that person could have owned the tract, with some slack for late recording.

  4. Filter hits by legal description, but always keep "all my interest in X County" conveyances and anything that cites an instrument already in the chain.

  5. Queue every new party for the same loop. Stop at the patent, the root-of-title date, or a prior opinion's cut-off. Fall back to legal-description indexes or the plant to catch misindexed instruments.

Guardrails

RiskRule
Scraping county portals and vendor sitesDon't. Their terms usually prohibit bots and bulk download, and getting around logins or rate limits creates contract and computer-crime exposure. Data licensesRead vendor terms for "no derivative datasets" and "no ML training" clauses. The GLO survey grid, the pyTRS PLSS parser and the Surya/Marker OCR weights all carry commercial-use limits.
Model driftPin model versions. Re-run the gold set on every model or prompt change. Store the prompt, model and schema version with every extracted value.

Recommended build: Python, FastAPI, Pydantic (one schema source for the LLM and for validation), PostgreSQL with PostGIS, pg_trgm and fuzzystrmatch, object storage with object lock for originals, a durable workflow queue, and a review screen that shows the page image with the field's box highlighted next to the extracted value.

Land system, mineral management and GIS

  • Mineral and royalty management: Enverus MineralSoft (revenue tracking, check reconciliation, valuation). This is the usual default for a minerals shop.
  • Land system of record: Quorum On Demand Land (small and mid-size), IFS BOLO/iLandMan (if accounting is on IFS), Pandell LandWorks with Whitestar (GIS-centric), PakEnergy Land (mid-market value), TotaLand (popular with brokers).
  • Grid: Whitestar Texas Original Land Survey grid (about 309,000 abstract polygons statewide) plus NM PLSS. Alternatives are IFS Tobin, the Enverus grid and US Land Grid.
  • GIS: QGIS (free) is enough for a small shop. Use ArcGIS Online or Enterprise once several people need shared ownership maps.
  • Owner contact and heirship: TLOxp or Accurint (needs a permissible-purpose credential), FamilySearch (free), Ancestry and Newspapers.com, Legacy.com obituaries, ClaimItTexas and NM unclaimed property, and DocuSign for ratifications and stipulations.

Recommended stacks

Minerals and land shop, 3–10 people
  • Use TexasFile for name searches and index export. Add CourthouseDirect TVP Plus for plant and legal-description searches and pre-modern images.
  • Use free county portals, BLM MLRS and NMSLO for the latest filings and for federal and state records.
  • Turn on Tracts.co day seats only during active projects. Move to monthly seats once usage is steady.
  • Use MineralSoft for the owned portfolio. Use QGIS with Whitestar for your area of interest, plus free RRC, GLO and BLM layers.
  • Build a thin in-house AI extraction layer (see Automation) on an enterprise LLM plan that doesn't train on your data.
Larger team or fund
  • Run Enverus Courthouse (plants, Instant Analyst, Runsheet Assistant) feeding Tracts.co.
  • Benchmark Energy Domain TitleLab against it as a second source, so you aren't dependent on one vendor.
  • Pick the system of record to match accounting: Quorum, IFS or Pandell.
  • Use Grooper or Collide to load acquisition data rooms. Use ArcGIS Enterprise for shared maps.
  • Give every broker a seat in your system, and set up central de-duplication of title already run, so nobody re-runs a tract the company already owns the work on.
Run a bake-off before you buy any AI title tool

Pick 20 tracts across 4 or 5 counties that already have attorney opinions: a mix of plant counties and late-index counties like Irion or Upton, and at least 3 tracts with heirship breaks. Give each vendor the same legal descriptions. Score them on:

  • Instrument recall: did they find every instrument the attorney examined?
  • Field accuracy: parties, dates, recording references and interest conveyed.
  • Final decimals: exact match to the opinion.
  • Hours of landman review per tract.

Missing an instrument is the expensive failure, so weight recall heaviest.

The operating model

  • In-house core: a land manager who owns the title product menu and the tracker; one or two senior title landmen who review and QC rather than abstract; a GIS/data analyst; and, if you build, one engineer who owns the pipeline and the fraction engine.
  • Contract brokers for courthouse-heavy counties (late-index counties, plant visits, heirship legwork). Give each one a seat in your system and your runsheet template so the work comes back in your format.
  • Title attorneys for drilling, division order and acquisition opinions, and for every legal-effect call the software flags.
  • One rule: before anyone opens an index, check the title register for existing work on that tract. Duplicate runs are the most common waste in land departments.

RolloutThe first 90 days

Days 1–30: Standardize
  • Adopt the five products, the runsheet schema, the file-naming key and the QC gate on this page.
  • Inventory every opinion, runsheet and abstract you own. Register each by tract with its certified-through date.
  • Open TexasFile and CourthouseDirect TVP Plus accounts. Get BLM Login.gov access for MLRS reports.
  • Load the GIS base: RRC surveys and wells, GLO and University Lands leases, BLM CadNSDI, NMSLO status, OCD wells.
  • Build and test the fraction engine. Pick 20 already-opined tracts as the gold set.
Days 31–60: Pilot
  • Run the vendor bake-off on the gold set: Enverus Courthouse + Tracts, Energy Domain TitleLab, and one young AI title tool.
  • Stand up the in-house extraction path (OCR + vision model + schema + review screen) on the same 20 tracts.
  • Score instrument recall, field accuracy, decimal match and review minutes per instrument.
  • Start the name-variant library and corporate-succession table.
Days 61–90: Production
  • Put live deals through the winning path with Tier A / Tier B review.
  • Turn on the KPI dashboard: cycle time, cost per tract, QC defects, auto-accept rate.
  • Start monthly pay-deck reconciliation against your computed decimals.
  • Launch the curative tracker with owners and due dates.
  • Make the build-vs-buy call based on the bake-off numbers, not on the demos.
